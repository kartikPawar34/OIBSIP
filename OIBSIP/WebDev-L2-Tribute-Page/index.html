Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam

The Missile Man & People's President of India (1931 – 2015)

A Life Dedicated to Science & Nation

Widely known for his pioneering work in the development of ballistic missile and launch vehicle technology, Dr. Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam dedicated his life to advancing India's scientific capabilities, strengthening national defence, and inspiring millions of youth around the world.

Early Life & Education

Born on October 15, 1931, in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, Kalam came from a humble background. His father was a boat owner, and young Kalam distributed newspapers to support his family's income. Despite financial struggles, his strong work ethic and passion for learning led him to study Physics at St. Joseph's College, Tiruchirappalli, and Aeronautical Engineering at the Madras Institute of Technology (MIT).

Major Contributions & Leadership

Space Program

Served as the Project Director for India's first indigenous Satellite Launch Vehicle (SLV-III), which successfully placed the Rohini satellite into orbit in 1980.

Missile Development

As Chief Executive of the Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme (IGMDP) at DRDO, he spearheaded the development and operationalization of strategic missiles including Agni and Prithvi.

Nuclear Tests

Played a critical organizational and technical role as Chief Scientific Adviser to the Prime Minister during the 1998 Pokhran-II nuclear tests, establishing India as a nuclear-weapon state.

Presidency

Served as the 11th President of India from 2002 to 2007. Beloved as the "People's President," he transformed the office into an accessible platform for youth interaction and national vision building.

Timeline of Milestones

  • 1931: Born in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu.
  • 1960: Joined the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) as a scientist.
  • 1969: Transferred to ISRO; became Project Director for India's first SLV-III.
  • 1981: Honored with the Padma Bhushan.
  • 1990: Honored with the Padma Vibhushan.
  • 1997: Awarded India's highest civilian honor, the Bharat Ratna.
  • 1998: Led technical execution of Pokhran-II nuclear tests.
  • 2002–2007: Served as the 11th President of India.
  • 2015: Passed away doing what he loved—lecturing students at IIM Shillong.

Highest Civilian Honors

Recognized nationally and internationally for his extraordinary contributions, he was awarded India's top civilian honors: Padma Bhushan (1981), Padma Vibhushan (1990), and the Bharat Ratna (1997).
